# Vaelora — Visual Narrative Language v0.3

This document owns how the world communicates history and culture visually without adding undocumented mechanics.

## 1. Narrative premise visible in art

Vaelora is rebuilding after a long decline in shared infrastructure and political cooperation. The visual world should therefore contain evidence of **routes, maintenance, reuse and local adaptation**, not evidence of a magical apocalypse.

The narrative tone is grounded:

- roads matter;
- settlements grow;
- old infrastructure survives unevenly;
- communities repair, reuse or abandon structures;
- natural environments have reclaimed neglected edges;
- conflict is territorial/economic/political as much as military.

## 2. The Trama as recurring motif

The Trama appears through:

- old route stones;
- signal/beacon foundations;
- repeated three-strand path geometry;
- bridge/arch language;
- worn civic markers;
- repair seams where newer materials meet older masonry.

Use it sparingly. A recurring symbol becomes stronger when it is not stamped on every asset.

## 3. Historical layers

Environmental props can suggest three visual ages:

### Current

Actively maintained roads, cities, faction banners, improvements and working infrastructure.

### Reused

Older masonry supporting newer timber; repaired markers; adapted route stones; old foundations integrated into present work.

### Abandoned

Broken beacon, eroded waystone or collapsed signal base partly reclaimed by vegetation.

These are visual layers only. They do not imply hidden rewards or archaeological actions.

## 4. Affordance safety

Presentation-only props must avoid common interaction cues:

- no glowing outline;
- no pulsing light;
- no obvious chest/container silhouette;
- no bright isolated rarity color;
- no hovering particles;
- no UI marker;
- no composition that blocks a unit or resource icon.

If a player reasonably asks “how do I click that?”, the prop is too gameplay-like.

## 5. Auren visual storytelling

Auren stories should be visible in **coordination**:

- aligned route markers;
- maintained public spaces;
- arches and civic crossings;
- readable civic centre;
- repairs that look standardized;
- ledgers/sign standards and beacon motifs.

Avoid making Auren look richer simply by adding gold. Their identity is organization, not luxury.

## 6. Brasal visual storytelling

Brasal stories should be visible in **adaptation and practical maintenance**:

- repaired iron straps;
- timber extensions over older stone;
- visible tool storage;
- kilns/work yards;
- modular walls/roofs;
- practical field packs and clan markers.

Avoid portraying repair as incompetence or decay. It should read as capable frontier craft.

## 7. Natural storytelling

Nature may tell small stories without becoming a mechanic:

- wind direction in coastal trees;
- vegetation accumulating near old stone;
- exposed rock on ridges;
- wetter plant density near coast/shallow water;
- sparse vegetation on severe peaks.

These variations must remain mechanically neutral.

## 8. Future civilizations

Elves, dwarves, goblins and skeletons are future scope. v0.3 may reserve visual-design questions but must not lock their canon or produce accepted assets. Their eventual art direction should extend Vaelora’s shared world language rather than overwrite terrain with faction-specific biomes.

## 9. Narrative review checklist

For each candidate ask:

- What does this object tell the player about the world?
- Is that statement already supported by canon?
- Could it be misread as a mechanic?
- Does the story come from structure/material/use rather than explanatory text?
- Does it strengthen Vaelora rather than generic fantasy?
